Several agents see one scene from viewpoints that do not line up, and nothing tells the model how they relate.

Abstract

Multimodal LLMs are usually evaluated on a single view, or on several views that share camera parameters. Collaborative settings are different. Several agents observe one environment from independent viewpoints, and a reliable decision needs reasoning across all of them. We introduce MaRiO, a benchmark for multi-agent collaborative reasoning, and evaluate 23 state of the art MLLMs on it. Static perception proves easier than composing geometric transformations, most models fail to resolve cross-agent correspondence as scenes grow more complex, and few exploit the geometric cues already present in the image. Geometry-guided synthetic views recover part of the gap, but not all of it.

Motivation

Earlier benchmarks hand the model a single coordinate frame

Single view. One perspective, so cross-view ambiguity cannot be resolved at all.

Multi view. Several images, but captured along one trajectory and usually shipped with poses, intrinsics or point clouds.

Multi agent. Each agent has its own egocentric frame and no shared metadata. Correspondence has to be established first, and only then can a spatial relation be read.

Input. Two or three synchronized views and a four-choice question. No camera poses, no intrinsics, no depth, no point clouds, no scene graphs.

Scenes. 913 real image sets from RealEstate10K and NAVI, plus 767 synthetic sets from VirtualHome, Habitat-Sim and AI2-THOR.

Tasks. Distance Awareness, Occlusion and Relative Position read spatial facts off the aligned views. Egocentric Motion and Navigation compose transformations between them.

Annotation. Three expert annotators wrote and cross-checked every question. An LLM only polished phrasing and drafted distractors, and every ground-truth label is human.

Results

Every model lands far below human accuracy

The gap. Humans reach 73.2. The strongest model, Gemini-2.5-Pro, reaches 55.7. The strongest open model, Ovis2.5-9B, reaches 41.2, against a random baseline of 23.9.

Where it hurts. Navigation and Egocentric Motion sit closest to random. Occlusion and Relative Position hold up best, so coarse spatial perception survives where viewpoint-dependent geometry does not.

Analysis

The failure is in matching views, not in seeing them

Perception does not transfer. Pooled over every model, compositional accuracy barely tracks static perceptual accuracy. The fit gives a slope of 0.15 and an R2 of 0.028.

Some models ignore the other view. Remove one observation, or all of them, and open-source accuracy hardly moves. On compositional tasks several stay above random with no image at all, which means the answer comes from option priors rather than from the scene.

A third agent hurts. Going from two agents to three lowers accuracy across almost every model and category. Each extra viewpoint enlarges the set of correspondences that must hold at once, and Navigation and Egocentric Motion suffer most.

Orientation is unstable. Flipping the images and swapping the direction words leaves a geometrically identical question, yet 22 to 32 percent of answers change. Bias ratios of 0.47 to 0.59 put every model near coin-flip fragility, and the drop is larger for directional questions than for obstacle ones, so the instability comes from the language cue.

Humans fail elsewhere. Of the 60 questions every evaluator answered correctly, Gemini-2.5-Pro missed 19. Of the 21 that tripped at least one person, it answered 11. The two error sets barely overlap, so the questions are answerable and the gap is real.

View augmentation

Synthetic in-between views help general models, not the spatial ones

  2. 2 Point cloud

The four stages of geometry-guided view synthesis, re-laid out from the single column used in the paper.

Method. Reconstruct a point cloud from the agent views, interpolate camera poses, render the frames in between, then let a diffusion video model turn them into novel views. They enter as extra visual context, not as another agent.

Effect. Qwen2.5-VL-7B gains 1.1 overall, with 4.5 on Occlusion and 3.9 on Relative Position. Spatial models gain nothing or lose ground, since they already infer geometry and the generated frames introduce small inconsistencies. Twelve frames works best, and cropped real frames do not reproduce the gain, so the benefit comes from the new viewpoints.
